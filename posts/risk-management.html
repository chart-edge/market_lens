RISK MANAGEMENT · STARTER ARTICLE

Why a risk plan comes before a profit target

A trade idea is incomplete until you know where it is wrong and how much you could lose.

This is an educational starter article, not a live market recommendation.

Define the invalidation point

Before entering a trade, identify the price or condition that would show the idea is no longer valid. Avoid choosing a stop only because it creates a convenient reward-to-risk ratio.

Size the position around risk

Position size should follow your predefined risk limit and the distance to your invalidation point. Consider fees, slippage, gaps, leverage, and the possibility that execution may be worse than expected.

Use a consistent checklist

  • What evidence supports the setup?
  • What would invalidate the idea?
  • What is the maximum planned loss?
  • Is the potential reward reasonable for the setup and market conditions?
A stop-loss can help define planned risk, but it cannot guarantee an exact exit price in fast-moving or illiquid markets.

Journal prompt

Record planned risk, actual risk, whether you followed your plan, and one specific improvement for your next review.
